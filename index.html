anti-pangram

A structural pattern checker for your own drafts — not a Pangram clone, not a predictor of its score.

What this actually does: Pangram's real detection model is proprietary and unreachable from outside — nothing here can reconstruct its actual reasoning. What it can do is flag a specific, well-documented pattern: paragraphs that read as mechanically uniform (often the ones synthesizing a bullet list, or written in tightly parallel technical prose). That structural uniformity is one of the most literature-backed signals AI detectors key on, so fixing it is a reasonable, honest way to reduce your real false-positive rate — even though it can't guarantee any specific Pangram verdict.
What gets checked, and why
  • Follows a bulleted/numbered list — paragraphs that immediately restate a preceding list in prose are the single most common false-positive trigger in technical writing.
  • Uniform sentence length — low variance in words-per-sentence (low "burstiness") across a paragraph, a classic statistical AI-text signal.
  • Repeated sentence openers — two or more sentences in the same paragraph starting with the same word ("This…", "This…"), a common LLM tic.
  • Generic connective phrases — stock transitions ("Together, these…", "It's worth noting…", "This ensures…") that carry no specific content.
  • Low concrete specificity — no numbers, proper nouns, contractions, or first-person markers anywhere in the paragraph — i.e. nothing a generic model couldn't have written about any topic.

Severity is a simple weighted count of these signals, shown as high/medium so you know where to spend editing time first — it is not a probability and is not calibrated against Pangram's own scoring.
